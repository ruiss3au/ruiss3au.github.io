The Calgary agenda reaches from the first information a device must decode to the radio components it can leave out. Radio Access Network Working Group 1 (RAN1) is considering broader control resources, simpler bandwidth signaling and requests that wake a sleeping network. Radio Access Network Working Group 4 (RAN4) has to determine how much of that flexibility survives transmitter constraints, receiver interruptions and reproducible testing. Several of these proposals reduce one burden by placing a new condition on access, scheduling or testing. [R1-2609858](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2609858.zip), [R1-2609626](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2609626.zip), [R4-2631947](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2631947.zip), [R4-2631857](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2631857.zip).

This is a pre-meeting account of proposals available before the 16-20 November RAN1#127 and RAN4#121 meetings. Company proposals remain proposals; a result under one device, channel or traffic assumption is not presented as a general 6G requirement. [R1-2609858](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2609858.zip), [R4-2631947](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2631947.zip).

## A wider control channel must still admit narrowband devices

A cell cannot rely on a device's later capability report to fix an initial-access format that the device cannot receive. Apple's downlink-control paper applies that constraint to the physical downlink control channel (PDCCH) scheduling the first system-information block (SIB1). It argues that a control resource set wider than the smallest maximum bandwidth of low-tier devices would prevent those devices accessing the cell, even if the wider transmission improved coverage for other equipment. [R1-2609858](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2609858.zip).

Apple proposes intra-slot control-channel repetition when the initial control resource set exceeds that lower-tier bandwidth. Samsung, meanwhile, supports full-band control resources and studies coarser frequency-domain resource indications for wide control bandwidths. These are different parts of the same design problem: how to use a large carrier efficiently while preserving the common entry path needed before the network knows which device has arrived. [R1-2609858](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2609858.zip), [R1-2609604](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2609604.zip).

### Removing signaling that deployment no longer needs

Apple's bandwidth paper questions whether 6G should retain the explicit common-grid offset used by New Radio (NR). It reports that the corresponding offset is rarely nonzero in its observed deployments and proposes making the first resource block of a single carrier the common-grid origin. Its motivation includes reducing mandatory SIB1 signaling. This is a proposal to remove a configuration degree of freedom, based on deployment evidence, rather than a claim that all legacy grid rules can simply disappear. [R1-2609857](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2609857.zip).

The decisions should be judged together. A wider control region may help one coverage condition while additional configuration bits or a wider minimum receiver burden another. The relevant evaluation needs the actual system-information size, the control repetition rule and the lowest-bandwidth receiver in the same setup. A gain measured only for a wideband receiver would leave the initial-access question unanswered. [R1-2609858](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2609858.zip), [R1-2609857](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2609857.zip).

## A wake-up request can spend the energy that sleep saves

An uplink wake-up signal lets a device request activity that a sleeping network has stopped providing continuously. The cost is a receiving path that listens for requests, device transmission and response monitoring, and the means to deliver the request configuration. Samsung therefore asks whether the additional energy-saving gain comes from the request itself or from a sparse broadcast configuration that would also work without it. [R1-2609626](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2609626.zip).

One of Samsung's evaluated cases makes the baseline issue concrete. Compared with a 20-millisecond SIB1 baseline, it attributes 28 percentage points of a 30.1% no-request network-energy gain to extending periodic SIB1 transmission to 160 milliseconds. Those savings cannot all be credited to wake-up signaling. In a different synchronization-adaptation case, the request reduces modeled access latency from 246.5 to 50.5 milliseconds, but consumes additional network and device energy. The two results describe distinct use cases, not one combined operating point. [R1-2609626](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2609626.zip).

Ericsson also favors narrowing the use cases. It proposes concentrating on idle and inactive devices requesting synchronization or system information, rather than connected-mode requests that overlap with mechanisms already under network control. It rejects wake-up-based adaptation of random-access resources on the grounds that the network already knows load and activity, and that additional device requests add overhead and false-alarm risk. Neither paper supports treating a new wake-up channel as automatically energy efficient. [R1-2610788](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2610788.zip).

The access and sleep discussions consequently share a prerequisite: the device needs enough timing and configuration to request the signals it lacks. A standalone cell that transmits nothing before the request creates a different feasibility problem from an assisted carrier. The next evaluation should preserve those deployment differences and compare the request against a practical sparse periodic baseline, with both network and device energy included. [R1-2609626](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2609626.zip), [R1-2610788](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2610788.zip).

## Removing a filter can give the scheduler a new constraint

The surface-acoustic-wave (SAW) filter discussion is about more than the minimum channel bandwidth. Removing filtering changes the protection offered to nearby bands and to the receiver. Nokia asks that a device be able to report implementation-specific uplink allocation restrictions and required power back-off. It argues that one worst-case back-off value across implementations could force the network to schedule every device against the most restrictive case. [R4-2630536](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2630536.zip).

Ericsson proposes moving the local oscillator to improve the placement of distortion products. Its evaluated benefit is allocation-dependent: switching helps near the ends of the resource-block range, but not in the middle. If retuning takes long enough to require guard time, Ericsson says the network should control it. Coarser retuning regions could reduce how often that guard is needed, at the cost of more power back-off. [R4-2631947](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2631947.zip).

### Legacy spectrum adds another alignment problem

Apple's multi-radio-access-technology spectrum-sharing paper shows why the freedom to move resources is limited by existing deployments. Aligning 5G and 6G resource grids can require changing carrier positions or existing aggregated-carrier arrangements. Apple identifies license-block edges as a constraint and asks operators about the feasibility of adjusting existing carrier-aggregation positions. Its proposed synchronization-raster simplification also has to preserve alignment where the generations share spectrum. [R4-2630492](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2630492.zip).

These are separate proposals, but they constrain the same act of scheduling a transmission. A filterless device may need a restricted allocation or a retuning guard; a shared-spectrum deployment may restrict where that allocation can move. Assessing component savings in isolation would miss the network information and scheduling freedom needed to realize them. Receiver blocking remains an additional check, not something a transmitter back-off calculation resolves. [R4-2630536](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2630536.zip), [R4-2631947](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2631947.zip), [R4-2630492](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2630492.zip).

## The scheduler needs to know when the receiver is absent

The measurement-gap debate asks how much freedom a device should have to retune its receiver while remaining connected. Nokia reports that its practical link adaptation treats interruption-related failures as evidence of poor channel conditions. The resulting reduction in transmission rate can persist beyond the short interruption. Its simulations also examine how an interrupted initial transmission can harm later retransmissions through the accumulated decoding state. [R4-2630391](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2630391.zip).

Ericsson finds that short interruptions known to the network perform well in its evaluated traffic scenarios. It also reports that a sufficiently short symbol-level autonomous interruption can approach that performance, while longer autonomous interruptions perform worse. The result supports comparing timing visibility and duration together, rather than choosing between an always-good autonomous design and an always-good configured gap. [R4-2631565](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2631565.zip).

Apple adds an implementation qualification: a device's measurement capability depends on the active serving-cell configuration and radio-component state, not just a nominal band combination. It proposes choosing symbol- or slot-level interruption case by case and postponing the affected-frequency granularity until the radio architecture is clearer. A capability claim that ignores which chains and tuner states are in use could promise measurements the current configuration cannot deliver. [R4-2630453](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2630453.zip).

This connects directly to oscillator retuning in filterless designs, without making the two procedures identical. In each case, an implementation-level transition can create a scheduling-visible absence. The relevant contract is what the network knows about affected carriers and timing, and how its feedback handling responds. Reporting only the fraction of receiver time lost would not capture those dependencies. [R4-2631947](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2631947.zip), [R4-2630453](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2630453.zip), [R4-2630391](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2630391.zip).

## A learned radio function needs a compatibility contract

Artificial intelligence and machine learning (AI/ML) appear in two quite different tasks here: compressing channel-state information (CSI) and predicting measurements that a device would otherwise make. In the compression case, a device encoder and network decoder have to interpret the same representation. Apple proposes limiting a lightweight applicability check to parameters that determine compatibility, including pairing identity, port configuration, subbands and payload size. It argues against adding unrelated inference settings to that check. [R1-2609841](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2609841.zip).

Samsung reports degradation when the network-side encoder assumption associated with an exchanged dataset differs from the device's encoder backbone. It proposes sharing that assumption as additional information, as well as aligning the pairing framework with deployment conditions. A standardized exchange format would therefore be insufficient if the parties still attach different model assumptions to the exchanged values. [R1-2609578](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2609578.zip).

RAN4's non-collocated cell-prediction study has a different dependency. The model must learn something about a target site from measurements at another site. Nokia says that correlated line-of-sight states do not establish the joint cross-site received-power correlation needed for that task. It also questions whether companies can reproduce one map-based ray-tracing setup, and proposes waiting for RAN1 guidance while giving other prediction cases priority. [R4-2631857](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2631857.zip).

The connection is not that both tasks need the same model. Both need a stated boundary between what is exchanged or assumed and what performance is promised. Compression needs an encoder that the intended decoder can use. Measurement prediction needs a channel relationship that survives the evaluation conditions. An attractive accuracy result in a matched setup does not establish either interoperability across implementations or generalization across deployments. [R1-2609578](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2609578.zip), [R1-2609841](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2609841.zip), [R4-2631857](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2631857.zip).

## Satellite compensation and interference models face different geometries

Non-terrestrial network (NTN) proposals reach beyond extending terrestrial coverage. Apple supports full network pre-compensation of feeder-link Doppler for a transparent satellite payload. Its argument is that residual feeder-link effects can increase the frequency error a device must handle, while coupling uplink frequency correction to timing-drift parameters can introduce quantization error into the frequency loop. This places the correction at a specific part of the link rather than treating all observed frequency error as a device problem. [R1-2609880](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2609880.zip).

Apple also supports feedback disabling or a sufficient number of parallel retransmission processes to address long satellite round trips. More processes can keep data moving while feedback travels, but require larger buffers; its proposal considers up to 32 for the geostationary case. This is another explicit allocation of complexity: removing feedback and adding parallel state have different effects on reliability handling and memory, and the paper does not present them as one interchangeable feature. [R1-2609880](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2609880.zip).

On coexistence, Ericsson's victim-network paper emphasizes the combined effects of satellite elevation, slant range and antenna pattern. It argues that a handheld antenna's effective polarization varies as both the device and interfering satellite geometry change, so a fixed polarization-loss assumption is not representative of the case it studies. Its proposed performance metric considers both average and lower-tail throughput loss. These remain company assumptions and proposals. [R4-2631693](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2631693.zip).

RAN1's compensation design and RAN4's protection study therefore need explicit deployment descriptions before their results can be combined. A device-frequency-error assumption does not define the distribution of aggregate interference, and a coexistence loss metric does not specify how the device obtains timing. The Calgary agenda has to preserve those separate dependencies while deciding which assumptions belong in common requirements and which remain implementation choices. [R1-2609880](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2609880.zip), [R4-2631693](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2631693.zip).
