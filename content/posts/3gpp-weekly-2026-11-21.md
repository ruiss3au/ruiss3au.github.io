The Calgary record contains progress on several fronts, with different kinds of closure. Radio Access Network Working Group 1 (RAN1) accepts the feasibility of a service indication in uplink scheduling and confirms reuse of existing mobility capabilities. It finds no physical-layer change specific to a digital-beamforming device architecture. Yet its answers on learned measurement prediction and network sensing leave substantial evaluation work unresolved. Those distinctions matter to Radio Access Network Working Group 4 (RAN4), where company papers translate the proposed functions into hardware, measurement and testing choices. The RAN4 implementation comparisons remain attributed to their authors; their inclusion here does not imply that Calgary adopted every proposed requirement. ## Service differentiation enters an already constrained control channel

RAN1's reply on service differentiation says an additional indication in uplink downlink control information (DCI) is feasible from the control-format perspective. The wording refers to logical channels served during logical-channel prioritization. That gives a physical-layer route to differentiate which traffic uses an uplink grant, rather than treating every grant as a resource allocation with no service context. [R1-2610953](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2610953.zip).

The reply also limits the design. RAN1 asks for the number of bits to be minimized unless justified, and places the field in a configured non-fallback uplink DCI format. It notes that this format can depend on device-specific radio resource control configuration. The response therefore establishes feasibility in a configured format; it does not specify an unlimited common service label that every device must decode before configuration. [R1-2610953](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2610953.zip).

That choice sits beside wider control-channel proposals. Samsung studies full-band control resources and larger frequency-allocation granularity, while Apple argues that initial control coverage must preserve access for the lowest-bandwidth devices. The parameters solve different problems, but each changes the control burden a receiver faces. Adding service information to a configured grant should not silently change the common initial-access assumptions. [R1-2609604](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2609604.zip), [R1-2609858](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2609858.zip), [R1-2610953](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2610953.zip).

The next useful evidence is a concrete field design in its actual control format: what behavior it selects, how configuration governs its presence, and what size or decoding cost it introduces. RAN1's reply answers whether the direction is possible. The balance between useful differentiation and control overhead still depends on the detailed design. [R1-2610953](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2610953.zip).

## Digital beamforming clears a protocol question rather than a cost test

RAN1 tells RAN4 that it has not identified physical-layer procedure or specification changes specific to a device's digital beamforming architecture in Frequency Range 2 (FR2). It does not specify that architecture and leaves it to implementation. This removes one potential reason to require a separate physical-layer procedure, without certifying the cost, power or measurement performance of a particular device design. [R1-2610995](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2610995.zip).

BeammWave's RAN4 contribution argues that its evaluated digital design does not require additional base-station hardware and is not expected to require mandatory software changes beyond the baseline. Before the reply, it explicitly made the software assessment subject to RAN1 feedback. Its implementation case includes a distributed architecture in which spatial processing occurs in the device. The later protocol response addresses that compatibility question, not every proprietary design assumption behind the implementation comparison. [R4-2630057](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2630057.zip), [R1-2610995](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2610995.zip).

### Component counts do not determine total cost

Murata supplies a more general caution. Digital beamforming adds converters, mixers and digital processing paths, but can remove analog beamformers and radio-frequency distribution losses. It argues that converter resolution, integration, oscillator architecture and the location of spatial filtering all change the balance. BeammWave proposes recording a favorable silicon-area comparison for its evaluated implementations; Murata says an architecture-level block count cannot establish that either analog or digital is generally cheaper. [R4-2630939](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2630939.zip), [R4-2630057](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2630057.zip).

The implementation freedom preserved by RAN1 makes that comparison more important. If several architectures fit the same procedures, the standard need not pick a winner through an unsupported universal cost claim. RAN4 can describe which receiver behavior a test assumes and which architecture assumptions underpin a result. A protocol-compatible implementation and an economically preferable implementation are separate conclusions. [R1-2610995](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2610995.zip), [R4-2630939](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2630939.zip), [R4-2630057](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2630057.zip).

## Learned prediction still lacks an agreed cross-site channel basis

RAN4's non-collocated prediction study seeks to infer a target cell's layer-three received power from measurements at a different site and frequency. Nokia warns that the model could learn the particular cross-site realization used in training and fail to generalize. Its proposal was to await RAN1's channel-model guidance rather than assume that a familiar model already supplied the required relationship. [R4-2631857](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2631857.zip).

RAN1's reply does not provide a ready replacement. It says it is not studying this channel-model case and does not recommend the three proposed options as readily applicable. Environmental objects developed for sensing were not designed to create inter-base-station channel correlations. Correlated line-of-sight states are not equivalent to channel spatial and frequency correlation. Map-based ray tracing adds cross-company alignment difficulties involving maps, materials and modeling. [R1-2610949](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2610949.zip).

The consequence for the prediction claim is specific. A model can produce a good score because the simulator generated a convenient relationship between its inputs and targets. Unless that relationship is both meaningful and reproducible, the score does not show how many real measurements can safely be omitted. Nokia's generalization concern and RAN1's modeling objections therefore identify a prerequisite to the claimed radio-resource-management benefit, rather than simply a preference for another simulator. [R4-2631857](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2631857.zip), [R1-2610949](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2610949.zip).

### Learned feedback also has to preserve its signal assumptions

A separate reply on learned channel-state information (CSI) compression reaches another testing boundary. RAN1 says an existing error vector magnitude metric can in principle be followed for the discussed option when modulation points have approximately equal probability and comparable average power. The unequal-probability case and the proper error limit require further study. Its text also says companies use their own projection matrices, encoders and target datasets. This is conditional guidance, not evidence that those implementations already share a common tested signal. [R1-2610977](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2610977.zip).

Apple's earlier interoperability proposal identifies compatibility parameters such as pairing identity, ports, subbands and payload size. That helps define what an encoder and decoder must agree on, but it does not resolve either the channel-model relationship for prediction or the waveform assumptions for a learned feedback signal. The Calgary work is separating these contracts; an accuracy number cannot stand in for all of them. [R1-2609841](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2609841.zip), [R1-2610949](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2610949.zip), [R1-2610977](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2610977.zip).

## Mobility capability reuse now has an explicit counting problem

The October request from RAN2 asked whether Release-18 and Release-19 Layer 1/Layer 2 Triggered Mobility (LTM) capabilities could cover both candidate special cells and candidate secondary cells. RAN1's Calgary reply has no concern with reuse and confirms that the listed capabilities, including their components, apply to the secondary-cell activation improvement. This is a concrete response to a dependency left open in the earlier discussion. [R4-2630009](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2630009.zip), [R1-2610954](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2610954.zip).

Reuse comes with accounting rules. RAN1 says existing value ranges are retained without extension. Per-cell components apply independently to each candidate special cell and secondary cell, while across-cell components cover the total across both roles. The maximum cell count also treats candidate special and secondary cells separately. These distinctions prevent a network from interpreting a per-cell capability as an unlimited shared pool. [R1-2610954](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2610954.zip).

The remaining ambiguity is a secondary cell configured under more than one candidate configuration. RAN1 leaves clarification of that case to RAN2. Counting the configuration references and counting the underlying cell are different operations; the reply identifies the case without resolving it. A network assembling several possible switches needs a consistent interpretation of that limit before relying on all of them simultaneously. [R1-2610954](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2610954.zip).

The work-item objective remains activation of secondary cells as part of the special-cell switch, with reuse of candidate measurement procedures where possible. The new reply advances capability alignment, but does not supply a measured interruption gain or settle every activation timing requirement. For implementers, progress is the narrower ability to apply known capability fields under explicit per-cell and shared limits, while tracking the unresolved shared-secondary-cell case. [RP-260232](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/TSG_RAN/TSGR_111/Docs/RP-260232.zip), [R1-2610954](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2610954.zip).

## More satellite synchronization beams need matching access windows

RAN1's outgoing satellite letter records discussion of eight synchronization-signal blocks (SSBs) below 3 GHz and a change to random-access occasion mapping based on the beam activation window. The issue is not only how many synchronization signals a satellite cell can expose. A device that discovers a beam needs random-access resources that are valid while that beam is actually serving it. [R1-2610984](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2610984.zip), [R1-2610934](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2610934.zip).

The moderator's earlier account explains the beam-hopping motivation. When a satellite serves footprints at different times, common-channel transmission and access occasions must be matched to those dwell intervals. The proposed active window has an offset, duration and periodicity; access occasions outside the window would not be valid. Those proposal details explain the mechanism, but the outgoing letter still marks activation-window configuration values as for further study. [R1-2610934](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2610934.zip), [R1-2610984](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2610984.zip).

The letter also identifies possible backward-compatibility issues with legacy satellite devices and changes in the radio-resource-management specification TS 38.133. RAN1 says the work might not align with the guidance for a technical enhancement item and asks plenary how to proceed. That makes the specification route part of the engineering issue: a small-looking signal-count extension can change device behavior and access timing across groups. [R1-2610984](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2610984.zip).

This is related to, but distinct from, the 6G compensation work described before Calgary. Apple's satellite proposal allocates feeder-link Doppler compensation to the network and makes assumptions about how the device obtains timing and satellite information. The Release-20 beam-hopping extension adds a scheduling window for when access is usable. Neither an available synchronization beam nor a corrected frequency alone establishes that an access opportunity is valid at the needed time. [R1-2609880](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2609880.zip), [R1-2610934](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2610934.zip), [R1-2610984](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2610984.zip).

## Sensing moves from a universal margin toward declared scenarios

RAN1 tells RAN4 it cannot recommend a generally applicable conversion from one radio's signal quality to network-wide sensing performance. Its reply lists deployment, sensing mode, node selection, target assumptions, detector processing, fusion and tracking as dependencies. It leaves scenario-specific characterization to RAN4 and says it does not currently plan to study a general mapping. This closes the expectation of receiving one universal conversion factor, rather than ending the sensing feasibility work. [R1-2610961](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2610961.zip).

CATT and CICTCI explain the geometry behind that answer: different sites can observe blind zones and help distinguish misleading reflections from additional targets. Ericsson's RAN1 contribution offers a narrower abstraction, checking whether the required number of ranked links exceeds a common detector threshold. That retains the availability of several observations, without pretending to reproduce the later object association and tracking result. [R1-2609650](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2609650.zip), [R1-2610364](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2610364.zip).

### Radio feasibility adds self-interference and switching dependencies

Ericsson's RAN4 feasibility paper discusses a different part of the chain. Full-duplex sensing must control leakage from its own transmitter; pulsed sensing introduces a blind zone determined by the pulse and switching behavior. The paper argues that another sensing mode or another node can cover a local blind zone, so one fixed switching-time limit would not represent all implementations. It proposes leaving synchronization choices open where relative over-the-air references can meet the task. [R4-2631809](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2631809.zip).

The two threads need to meet in an aligned evaluation. Passing a radio leakage or switching test does not prove that the selected set of observations produces acceptable detections. Conversely, a network result using helpful additional nodes does not automatically establish that each radio can meet its assumed input conditions. A scenario-specific test should keep the node geometry, detector settings and hardware assumptions visible from the RF measurement through the object decision. [R1-2610961](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2610961.zip), [R1-2610364](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2610364.zip), [R4-2631809](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2631809.zip).
