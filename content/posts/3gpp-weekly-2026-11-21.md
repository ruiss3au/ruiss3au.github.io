A stronger target echo does not necessarily solve a sensing network's detection problem. Multiple radios can contribute different views of an object, reject misleading reflections and cover one another's blind zones. Treating those benefits as a fixed improvement in one radio's signal quality can erase the mechanism responsible for them. November evidence from the Third Generation Partnership Project (3GPP) supports a narrower, useful intermediate test: measure whether enough sensing links remain usable under interference, while keeping the final object-detection claim separate. [R1-2609650](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2609650.zip), [R1-2610364](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2610364.zip).

The new reply from Radio Access Network Working Group 1 (RAN1), headed Calgary, 16-20 November 2026, says it cannot recommend a generally applicable conversion between single-node and network sensing performance. It leaves room for a scenario-specific mapping in the radio-frequency (RF) feasibility and coexistence work of Radio Access Network Working Group 4 (RAN4). The distinction matters for evaluators: a repeatable test of radio impairment needs a declared relationship to network performance, rather than an assumed universal fusion gain. [R1-2610961](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2610961.zip).

## The baseline: a network target, a single-radio test

RAN4's October request identified the mismatch. The sensing performance indicators in Technical Report 38.914 apply at network level, while RAN4 says it can define and test RF performance only for a single base station. It proposed studying single-base-station sensing feasibility and coexistence as a baseline, and asked whether a fusion gain or scaling factor could connect that work to network targets. It had made no decision on that approach. This is the earlier question covered in the [October meeting report](/3gpp/ran4-120bis/); November's contribution is the answer and the engineering alternatives behind it. [R1-2609012](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2609012.zip).

Signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) expresses the strength of a target echo relative to noise. Signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio (SINR) also accounts for interfering transmissions. These are useful inputs to a sensing detector, but the network's result comes later. Ericsson describes a chain of per-radio processing, detection, optional combination of observations from multiple radios, object formation and association with the actual simulated targets. Detection and false-alarm performance are calculated after that chain. A signal-quality threshold at its entrance therefore cannot, by itself, specify everything that happens at its exit. [R1-2609486](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2609486.zip), [R1-2610364](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2610364.zip).

## More power does not supply another viewpoint

CATT and CICTCI identify two reasons why the gap is more than a missing power margin. In their account of the earlier drone-sensing study, non-line-of-sight reflections from moving targets can have nonzero Doppler shifts and be mistaken for additional objects. A radio also has angular blind zones, including the area above a transmission and reception point (TRP), the radio site making the observation. Other sites can help reject the misleading paths and observe targets the first site misses. [R1-2609650](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2609650.zip).

That creates a causal distinction. Improving an echo's strength can help a detector see it, but does not by itself establish whether that echo belongs to a real additional object. Nor does it provide a second viewing angle. CATT and CICTCI report that the false-alarm and missed-detection problems they discuss change relatively little with base-station transmit power. Their explanation supports treating spatial confirmation and visibility as separate dependencies, rather than representing every network benefit with extra SNR. [R1-2609650](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2609650.zip).

This does not make multiple sites mandatory for every successful sensing implementation. The same paper says temporal tracking can improve single-node performance by using observations over time. It also says tracking is optional in the studies and that its fusion benefit may differ from detection alone. A mapping calibrated for one-shot detection consequently needs another check before being used for tracking. [R1-2609650](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2609650.zip).

## What a simplified test chooses to preserve

The company contributions agree that a universal conversion is unavailable, but preserve different parts of the network when simplifying it. Their alternatives make different promises. [R1-2609482](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2609482.zip), [R1-2610364](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2610364.zip), [R1-2609486](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2609486.zip).

ZTE and Sanechips' zero-decibel proposal is conditional: if the network uses strongest-echo selection as its fusion method, there is no additional fusion improvement to account for. That is a definition of the simplified network, not a measurement showing that combining several observations never helps. Against CATT and CICTCI's explanation, the cost is clear: a test using that rule cannot assess the benefit of cross-node rejection of misleading reflections. [R1-2609482](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2609482.zip), [R1-2609650](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2609650.zip).

Ericsson retains one additional dependency without fixing the complete fusion algorithm. For each target and simulated realization, calculate the candidate links' SNR without adjacent-channel interference and SINR with it. Rank the links in each case and retain the nth-best value. If that value exceeds the detector threshold, at least n links exceed it. Over many targets and realizations, the fraction above the threshold measures availability of the required number of usable observations. [R1-2610364](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2610364.zip).

For a two-link confirmation rule, this means checking the second-best link. An excellent strongest link cannot compensate for the second link falling below threshold. This is the useful distinction between the selection and availability approaches: the latter can expose interference that removes corroborating observations even when the best observation survives. It follows from Ericsson's ranking rule and its description of implementations requiring confirmation by multiple TRPs; it is an analytical consequence, not a reported comparison between the proposals. [R1-2610364](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2610364.zip).

The retained link count still does not certify a detected object. Ericsson explicitly excludes the subsequent clustering, fusion, association and tracking gains from this abstraction. It also requires a common detector configuration: the searched delay, Doppler and angle bins, sensing resources, processing interval, and detection and false-alarm criteria affect the threshold. Leaving those settings unspecified would move the uncertainty into the supposedly common threshold. [R1-2610364](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2610364.zip).

## A calibrated threshold is possible, but carries its model

vivo supplies the strongest qualification to an overly broad claim that no mapping is possible. It reports a six-TRP simulation with network fusion that meets its stated detection and false-alarm targets at approximately -20.6 decibels of sensing SNR, corresponding to approximately 23.6 decibels after processing. Its single-TRP case cannot meet the network targets within the evaluated range. It proposes deriving a single-base-station requirement from the network simulation's successful operating point. [R1-2609486](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2609486.zip).

The definition of that operating point matters as much as its value. vivo defines multi-TRP sensing SNR as an arithmetic mean over the SNR samples from all six TRPs. The underlying samples compare target-echo power with noise on one resource element and one antenna port; post-processing SNR additionally includes the benefit of combining observations over the sensing resources. Its reported mean therefore does not say that every individual radio has the same margin, or that the required number of links clears a detector threshold for every target. Reading it as a portable single-radio limit would discard the distribution that the full simulation used. [R1-2609486](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2609486.zip).

This is a different evaluation bargain from Ericsson's. Full simulation preserves the processing that turns observations into objects; the ranked-link approach exposes availability without reproducing that processing. Neither paper establishes a matched numerical comparison between them. vivo's extracted text gives the operating point and SNR definition, but omits the detailed assumption table and plotted curves, so it cannot establish a transferable RF limit here. RAN1's final reply is consistent with this distinction: it rejects a generally applicable mapping while leaving scenario-specific characterization to RAN4. It does not select either company's method. [R1-2609486](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2609486.zip), [R1-2610364](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2610364.zip), [R1-2610961](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2610961.zip).

## The decisive test is whether the abstraction predicts the loss

For a coexistence evaluator, the immediate opportunity is a controlled comparison. Ericsson proposes applying the same detector threshold and link-count criterion with and without adjacent-channel interference. That measures how interference changes access to usable sensing observations under the chosen deployment. It supplies a repeatable intermediate result without assuming that additional power and additional viewpoints are interchangeable. [R1-2610364](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2610364.zip).

The next discriminating experiment should run that availability test alongside complete object processing on the same targets, geometry and radio resources. Compare strongest-echo selection, the declared multi-link criterion and the full fusion result, then introduce the same interference. Check missed detections, false alarms and position errors separately, and repeat with tracking if tracking is claimed. This proposed test follows from the dependencies identified by Ericsson, CATT and CICTCI, and vivo. If link availability tracks the object-level degradation, it can support a calibrated coexistence methodology. If usable-link counts remain healthy while object decisions fail, the abstraction needs the missing geometry or processing dependency before it can justify a network performance claim. [R1-2610364](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2610364.zip), [R1-2609650](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2609650.zip), [R1-2609486](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_127/Docs/R1-2609486.zip).
