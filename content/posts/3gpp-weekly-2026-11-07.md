Samsung proposes transmitter error vector magnitude (EVM) limits of 2.5% at 4 GHz and 2.8% at 7 GHz for fixed wireless access (FWA) uplink 1024-point quadrature amplitude modulation (1024-QAM). [R4-2630294](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2630294.zip)

Ericsson instead proposes 2.5% at both frequencies. Its submission puts a competing option before Radio Access Network Working Group 4 (RAN4): one transmitter-error limit across both frequencies. [R4-2631347](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2631347.zip)

Samsung’s paper is a discussion submission for Calgary on 16-20 November 2026. [R4-2630294](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2630294.zip)

Ericsson’s paper is also a discussion submission for that meeting. These are proposals for the forthcoming meeting, not agreed November outcomes. [R4-2631347](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2631347.zip)

## Samsung weighs recovered power against modulation gain

Samsung compares the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) at which 1024-QAM begins to outperform 256-QAM with the output power recovered by relaxing transmitter EVM. It argues that each candidate limit should be judged by comparing the change in crossover SNR with the change in power back-off. [R4-2630294](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2630294.zip)

For 4 GHz, Samsung reports that relaxing transmitter EVM from 2.5% to 2.8% raises the crossover SNR by about 3 decibels in the single-layer cases it discusses. Under its assumed distortion budget, it estimates only about 0.9 decibels of recovered output power, and argues that the relaxation reduces the net gain. [R4-2630294](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2630294.zip)

At 7 GHz, Samsung argues for 2.8% to accommodate larger power-amplifier and local-oscillator contributions while preserving more modulation gain than 3%. Its calculation assumes about 1.2% local-oscillator phase noise. [R4-2630294](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2630294.zip)

## Ericsson seeks one limit and fewer test variants

Ericsson argues that one EVM limit avoids separate maximum power reduction (MPR) sets and a frequency boundary. It says a frequency-dependent limit would require two MPR sets and corresponding conformance tests, whereas one limit would allow a single set per device power class and waveform. [R4-2631347](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2631347.zip)

Ericsson also proposes leaving the number of multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) layers unrestricted. It argues that link adaptation selects transmission rank and modulation together, so multilayer transmission need not be scheduled when the channel or base-station configuration cannot support it. [R4-2631347](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2631347.zip)

## Error limits precede the final power requirement

Samsung says that choosing transmitter EVM during the study sets the power-amplifier linearity needed and hence constrains the power reduction to be specified later. [R4-2630294](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2630294.zip)

The exact MPR, Samsung says, will be decided in the work-item phase. [R4-2630294](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2630294.zip)
