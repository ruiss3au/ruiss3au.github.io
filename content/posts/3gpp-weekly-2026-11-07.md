A transmitter can send a cleaner radio signal by reducing its output power. It can also send a stronger signal with more distortion. The 6G fixed-wireless uplink question is which choice leaves the base station with more useful throughput. Samsung and Ericsson's November submissions propose different error limits at 7 GHz, but their more important disagreement concerns how to judge that balance. [R4-2630294](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2630294.zip), [R4-2631347](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2631347.zip).

Samsung proposes 2.5% transmitter error vector magnitude at 4 GHz and 2.8% at 7 GHz. Ericsson proposes 2.5% for both. These are discussion papers for the 16-20 November Calgary meeting of Radio Access Network Working Group 4 (RAN4). They give enough evidence to examine the competing choices, but do not establish an adopted 6G requirement. [R4-2630294](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2630294.zip), [R4-2631347](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2631347.zip).

## What the percentage controls

Quadrature amplitude modulation (QAM) represents data through combinations of signal amplitude and phase. Adding more possible symbols packs those combinations closer together, making errors in the transmitted signal more consequential. Error vector magnitude (EVM) measures the displacement between an intended symbol and the measured one. A lower limit demands a more accurate signal; a larger allowance accepts more error. [Rohde & Schwarz's technical explanation](https://www.rohde-schwarz.com/us/products/test-and-measurement/analyzers/signal-and-spectrum-analyzers/understanding-error-vector-magnitude_258370.html) describes this relationship.

For this decision, the distortion budget includes more than the power amplifier. Samsung discusses amplifier distortion, transceiver nonlinearity, local-oscillator phase noise and in-phase/quadrature imbalance. Its argument is that reducing output power mainly improves the amplifier contribution; it does not remove the other errors at the same rate. A looser overall EVM limit therefore cannot be translated directly into an equally large improvement in received signal quality. [R4-2630294](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2630294.zip).

Power back-off is the reduction in amplifier operating power used to meet the signal-quality constraint. The requirements discussion expresses this power cost through maximum power reduction (MPR). Samsung argues that choosing EVM during the study already constrains the amplifier linearity and the MPR that will be specified later. Deferring the final power requirement does not make the current distortion decision independent of power. [R4-2630294](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2630294.zip).

## At 4 GHz, the extra power does not repay the penalty

Samsung uses crossover signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) as its link-performance test: the point where uplink 1024-QAM starts to deliver more throughput than 256-QAM. A higher crossover means the link needs better conditions before the higher-order modulation earns its place. In the single-layer results Samsung discusses at 4 GHz, relaxing transmitter EVM from 2.5% to 2.8% raises that crossover by roughly 3 decibels. [R4-2630294](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2630294.zip).

Its hardware calculation gives only about 0.9 decibels of recovered output power for the same relaxation. Using Samsung's estimates, the roughly 3-decibel increase in required SNR exceeds the potential 0.9-decibel received-SNR benefit by about 2.1 decibels. This subtraction illustrates the disadvantage under those assumptions; it is not a universal margin for every channel or transmitter. [R4-2630294](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2630294.zip).

That example explains why allowing a less accurate transmitter can make a higher modulation order harder to exploit. The link gets somewhat more power, but its throughput advantage begins at a substantially better channel condition. Both November papers support 2.5% at 4 GHz. Their proposed limits diverge at 7 GHz. [R4-2630294](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2630294.zip), [R4-2631347](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2631347.zip).

## At 7 GHz, the hardware budget and the evidence diverge

Samsung assumes a larger local-oscillator phase-noise contribution at 7 GHz: about 1.2%, compared with about 1% at 4 GHz. In its calculation, holding overall EVM at 2.5% leaves an amplifier contribution of about 1.7%, tighter than its roughly 1.8% allowance at 4 GHz. It estimates about one decibel of additional back-off compared with a 2.8% limit. Its proposed 2.8% consequently makes room for both oscillator and amplifier impairments. [R4-2630294](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2630294.zip).

Ericsson supplies a different part of the evidence. It reports a 7 GHz evaluation with 2.5% transmitter and receiver EVM, explicitly without phase noise. It says the higher modulation reaches 90% of its maximum throughput in the configurations identified in its text. That establishes a reported link-level result under the stated simulation assumptions. It does not, by itself, close Samsung's question about output power with a practical oscillator and amplifier budget. [R4-2631347](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2631347.zip), [R4-2630294](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2630294.zip).

There is also a difference in the success criterion. Samsung asks when 1024-QAM first outperforms 256-QAM. Ericsson reports SNR at 90% of each modulation-and-coding scheme's maximum throughput. Crossing an existing mode and approaching a mode's own maximum answer different questions. Their SNR figures cannot be treated as competing estimates of one identical threshold. [R4-2630294](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2630294.zip), [R4-2631347](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2631347.zip).

The evaluation record also needs reconciliation. Samsung says 2.5% was absent from the RAN4 7 GHz link-level campaign; Ericsson says its earlier 7 GHz evaluation did use it. Those statements may concern different evaluation sets. Ericsson's extracted text also names one channel model in its setup description and another in its numerical discussion. The original simulation material would need checking before aligning individual result points across the submissions. [R4-2630294](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2630294.zip), [R4-2631347](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2631347.zip).

## One limit simplifies the requirements, but still needs a power case

Ericsson's case for 2.5% is also a requirements-design argument. It says a frequency-dependent limit would require separate MPR sets, corresponding conformance tests and a boundary between frequency categories. It points to the difficulty of applying a 4.2 GHz boundary to the 4400-4800 MHz range. One EVM value would permit one MPR set per device power class and waveform across bands. [R4-2631347](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2631347.zip).

This creates a tradeoff between a uniform requirements structure and the allowance for frequency-dependent hardware impairments. Ericsson argues that the added power reduction for 2.5% is limited; Samsung estimates a different cost when it budgets the 7 GHz amplifier and oscillator contributions. The available text supports comparing those assumptions, rather than declaring either percentage intrinsically more efficient. [R4-2631347](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2631347.zip), [R4-2630294](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2630294.zip).

## The receiver is part of the feasibility claim

Earlier evidence from LG Electronics makes clear why transmitter EVM cannot stand in for the entire link. Its May RAN4#119 contribution reports that additional receive antennas lower crossover SNR in its evaluated configurations, while some four-layer cases never exceed 256-QAM throughput within the simulated range. LG proposes feasibility for one- and two-layer operation with enough receive antennas. These are conditional results, not a general guarantee for every base station. [R4-2605706](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_119/Docs/R4-2605706.zip).

Ericsson uses the receiver dependency to argue against a fixed layer restriction. It says the network selects transmission rank and modulation together, and can refrain from scheduling multiple layers when the channel or base-station configuration cannot support them. Its proposal shifts that choice into link adaptation. The practical comparison must therefore state the receiver configuration and number of layers as well as transmitter error and power. [R4-2631347](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2631347.zip).

If the stricter limit delivers a useful throughput advantage at the output power that a practical 7 GHz transmitter can sustain, the single-limit approach avoids an additional requirements split. If its amplifier and oscillator budget forces enough power reduction to erase that advantage, an allowance for 7 GHz hardware has a stronger case. The common comparison still needed is useful throughput at a practical transmit-power operating point, with matched receiver and impairment assumptions. [R4-2631347](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2631347.zip), [R4-2630294](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2630294.zip), [R4-2605706](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_119/Docs/R4-2605706.zip).

A follow-up evaluation should place the candidate EVM limits on the same throughput-versus-link-budget comparison, include the oscillator and amplifier errors consistently, and state both crossover and near-maximum-throughput criteria. The unresolved choice is how much practical output power each distortion allowance costs, and whether the resulting link retains the gain used to justify 1024-QAM. [R4-2630294](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2630294.zip), [R4-2631347](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_121/Docs/R4-2631347.zip).
