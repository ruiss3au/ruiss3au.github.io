An efficient waveform is not necessarily an efficient transmitter. A phone
that measures a neighboring cell without a configured measurement gap may
still interrupt its serving connection. An artificial-intelligence (AI) model
that performs well against its training assumptions may fail on another
vendor's hardware. These different engineering problems converge on a
question running through the August RAN4 contributions: what must be
demonstrated before a capability becomes a dependable radio requirement?

Skyworks challenges amplifier models used to evaluate waveform gains;
Qualcomm examines scheduling costs hidden inside gap-less measurements;
Apple asks how learned radio functions should be validated after deployment.
Alongside these 6G studies, Release-20 work confronts practical limits in
base-station coexistence, AI test equipment and Ambient Internet of Things
(IoT) radios
[R4-2612064](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_120/Docs/R4-2612064.zip),
[R4-2612117](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_120/Docs/R4-2612117.zip),
[R4-2610597](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_120/Docs/R4-2610597.zip).

At Maastricht, held on 24-28 August, the Radio Access Network working
group responsible for radio performance and conformance (RAN4) was working
across two horizons: requirements for Release-20 features and the foundations
of the next generation. The connection between them is practical. More
flexible operation needs measurable limits on hardware behavior, interference
and implementation complexity.

This analysis distinguishes company proposals from decisions. The reviewed
primary documents do not establish a complete set of final August
dispositions. Earlier agreements reproduced in contributions are not treated
as new outcomes, and the closing watchlist identifies what remains to be
verified.

## Spectrum continuity: a new generation must use existing holdings

### Combinations on day one

A broad operator contribution, including T-Mobile, Deutsche Telekom,
Vodafone, Orange, AT&T and Verizon, asks for combinations deployed in 5G to
be available in 6G from day one, based on operator requests. The
qualification matters: this is not an instruction to copy every historical
combination automatically. It is a demand that migration should not remove
the ability to aggregate spectrum operators already use
[R4-2612126](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_120/Docs/R4-2612126.zip).

The authors also address maximum sensitivity degradation (MSD), the
receiver allowance associated with difficult band combinations. If RAN4
defines MSD for 6G combinations, they propose reusing 5G values unless
6G-specific RF characteristics justify different requirements. Capability
signaling could distinguish improved behavior without delaying the initial
combination.

The tradeoff is between availability and improvement. Requiring every
combination to complete a fresh optimization exercise could impede
migration; copying every allowance indefinitely could surrender achievable
receiver performance. The operators propose separating initial availability
from subsequently signaled improvements. Whether that separation becomes
policy still needs a verified disposition.

### Sharing requires physical alignment

Another joint contribution, from operators and Ericsson, seeks aligned
resource-block boundaries between New Radio (NR) and 6G carriers supporting
multi-radio-access-technology spectrum sharing (MRSS). It also proposes
allowing the 7.5 kHz uplink half-subcarrier offset inherited from Long Term
Evolution (LTE). The authors connect alignment to time/frequency sharing,
control-channel reuse and infrastructure reuse
[R4-2611987](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_120/Docs/R4-2611987.zip).

T-Mobile, Deutsche Telekom, TELUS, Bell Mobility, Anterix and Rogers extend
the deployment question to partially overlapping carriers and asymmetric
uplink/downlink bandwidths. Their guard-band proposal is conditional:
where 5G minimum guard bands are at least as large as 6G's, the 5G values
could be used for shared operation
[R4-2612124](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_120/Docs/R4-2612124.zip).

These papers make migration an RF design constraint, not merely a later
rollout choice. Flexible bandwidths are useful only if the resulting
carrier can share the spectrum actually available. This connects RAN4's
parameters to RAN1's spectrum-sharing work without establishing acceptance
of these particular proposals.

## Waveforms: power gains must survive the hardware

Skyworks reports promising low peak-to-average-power-ratio (PAPR) waveform
measurements, but its important warning concerns the evaluation method. A
power-amplifier (PA) model extracted using a high-PAPR signal may not
represent saturation, electrical supply or thermal behavior when a low-PAPR
waveform is driven harder. Matching amplitude and phase curves is not enough
to establish sustainable power boosting
[R4-2612064](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_120/Docs/R4-2612064.zip).

Its measurement caveats are substantial: smaller channel bandwidths,
particular allocations and calibration, and verification of out-of-band
requirements rather than every signal-quality constraint. Some prospective
improvements still need checks of in-band emissions, error-vector magnitude
and spectrum flatness. Reporting headline power increases as an established
coverage gain would discard the qualifications the authors want considered.

Ericsson evaluates frequency-domain spectral shaping, with and without
spectrum extension, in a relative comparison using 5G assumptions. It finds
benefits depend on modulation, allocation and code rate; for quadrature
phase-shift keying and inner allocations, reported advantages are modest.
Additional benefit from non-transparent rather than transparent shaping is
also small in its evaluated cases
[R4-2612121](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_120/Docs/R4-2612121.zip).

These are not interchangeable experiments. One emphasizes measured
amplifier behavior and model limitations; the other evaluates net benefit
under specified signal assumptions. A useful next comparison must align
power limits, occupied spectrum, signal quality and receiver treatment.
The question is not simply which waveform has the lowest PAPR, but which
improvement survives its hardware and bandwidth costs.

## Mobility: gap-less does not mean interruption-free

### Who controls the interruption?

Apple separates gap-less measurement into autonomous interruptions,
interruptions known to both network and device, and operation without
interruption. It proposes considering the choice case by case. A user
equipment (UE) capability to measure another carrier does not itself
establish that the serving connection remains uninterrupted. Antenna tuning
and RF-component state may also make measurement performance differ between
carrier-aggregation operation and a single-carrier measurement gap
[R4-2610563](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_120/Docs/R4-2610563.zip).

Qualcomm makes the network-side cost explicit. Unrestricted autonomous
interruptions can create scheduling uncertainty, retransmissions and
throughput loss. Device-indicated interruption locations can instead add
signaling and scheduling complexity. It favors prioritizing a network-aware
approach with interruptions placed around synchronization-signal measurement
occasions, while retaining autonomous operation as a study possibility
[R4-2612117](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_120/Docs/R4-2612117.zip).

Timing granularity exposes a difference in emphasis. Apple asks RAN4 to
consider both symbols and slots case by case. Qualcomm proposes assuming
slots for now, revisiting symbols when actual interruption duration and
scheduling benefit are clearer. It also warns that visible interruption
includes non-overlapping software and firmware processing, not just RF
switching. Hardware speed alone cannot settle the requirement.

### Unification must preserve connection reliability

NTT DOCOMO asks for unified measurements to maintain accuracy and
reliability at least equivalent to 5G, prioritize link recovery when tasks
collide, and impose a maximum delay on deprioritized measurement objects.
It wants explicit accuracy and filtering conditions before physical-layer
measurements are reused for higher-layer mobility decisions
[R4-2610791](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_120/Docs/R4-2610791.zip).

Fewer measurement mechanisms therefore do not automatically mean a simpler
operational contract. The framework must still tell the network which tasks
can wait, which keep the connection alive, and which samples are suitable
for a mobility decision. This is a terrestrial reliability debate,
independent of satellite handover.

## Receiver testing: reproducibility before ambitious thresholds

Apple challenges premature assumptions about advanced receiver baselines.
Reduced-complexity maximum-likelihood receivers are a family of algorithms,
not one reproducible implementation. It asks RAN4 to finish comparing that
family with the minimum-mean-square-error interference-rejection-combining
baseline before deciding the consequences of multiple baselines
[R4-2610584](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_120/Docs/R4-2610584.zip).

The same paper insists that transmitter error-vector-magnitude assumptions
for demodulation tests should derive from achievable base-station RF
performance. A test generator's capability is no substitute for the signal
quality a deployed base station can deliver. Receiver targets must connect
to the RF study rather than assume ideal inputs.

Nokia raises a complementary problem: combining channel-state-information
(CSI) reporting checks into a link-adaptation test can hide which reporting
function failed. It wants study of whether detailed rank and channel-quality
performance can still be identified; otherwise dedicated requirements
should remain. For demodulation it proposes investigating whether companies
can align at a 90% throughput test point, retaining 70% as a fallback
[R4-2610613](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_120/Docs/R4-2610613.zip).

This does not establish an Apple-Nokia confrontation. The papers identify
different weaknesses in the path from simulation to conformance: uncertain
receiver complexity, unrealistic transmitter inputs, and tests difficult
to align or diagnose. Stronger numerical requirements are useful only when
the test is reproducible and failure means something specific.

## AI: interoperability and failure handling

### Release-20 compression meets the test platform

The Release-20 two-sided CSI-compression work makes interoperability
concrete. Ericsson proposes specifying reference encoder and test decoder
structures through mathematics and data-flow graphs, with weights in a
separate numerical file, rather than using a model format such as Open
Neural Network Exchange (ONNX) as the specification. It also asks for joint
evaluation of encoder payload adaptation and corresponding decoder behavior
[R4-2611534](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_120/Docs/R4-2611534.zip).

Rohde & Schwarz introduces the test-platform constraint. Its measurements
indicate that using the maximum supported model complexity would leave
inadequate headroom for additional testing functions. It recommends against
consuming that maximum and considers standardized quantization premature
without evidence that different implementations change UE test results.
An informative record of quantization options is offered as an alternative
[R4-2611770](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_120/Docs/R4-2611770.zip).

The issue is not only compression accuracy. RAN4 must identify what has to
be identical across vendors and what can remain implementation choice,
while fitting a test system that must do more than execute the model.
Neither paper establishes adoption of its preferred representation or
complexity budget.

### 6G models outside their training conditions

For digital post-distortion, where receiver processing compensates
transmitter nonlinearity, Apple asks for explicit training/evaluation
mismatch. At least one amplifier model, device type or operating condition
should be withheld from training. Matched-condition AI, mismatched-condition
AI and a non-AI baseline should be compared under the same conditions
[R4-2610598](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_120/Docs/R4-2610598.zip).

That matters particularly if compensation justifies relaxed transmitter
requirements. Apple proposes reporting generalization coverage alongside
net gain and studying fallback to legacy transmitter requirements when
compensation is inadequate. Otherwise, a receiving algorithm could be
asked to carry a hardware concession without evidence of where that bargain
continues to work.

Its monitoring contribution proposes parallel evaluation of an updated
model before it affects live radio decisions, possible notification of
relevant UE-side model changes, and continued-use, restriction or fallback
decisions based on observed performance
[R4-2610597](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_120/Docs/R4-2610597.zip).

These proposals turn model lifecycle into a radio-performance problem.
The next useful result is not simply another average gain: it is a defined
evaluation domain, an observable failure condition and a workable response.

## Release-20 terrestrial work: isolation and shared hardware

Sub-band full duplex (SBFD) base-station coexistence exposes a different
implementation limit. Nokia's simulations lead it to propose a 40 dB
improvement in adjacent-channel interference ratio over its stated baseline
to keep cell-edge uplink throughput degradation below 5%. Those values
belong to its simulation assumptions, not an adopted universal requirement
[R4-2612012](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_120/Docs/R4-2612012.zip).

Ericsson likewise finds substantial isolation needs, but stresses that
analog filtering adds insertion loss and raises receiver noise figure.
For side-by-side deployment it asks which percentile of coupling loss
should represent isolation. A more protective assumption changes the
filtering burden; filtering then changes the receiver being protected
[R4-2612047](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_120/Docs/R4-2612047.zip).

Ambient IoT brings shared hardware into the handset. Apple proposes reusing
appropriate NR transmitter requirements for an intermediate UE acting as a
reader, while identifying separate switching, reception and interference
questions. It does not want that role restricted to a 23 dBm power class.
Emission and selectivity requirements still depend on coexistence work;
some receiver requirements depend on RAN1's physical design
[R4-2610591](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_120/Docs/R4-2610591.zip).

Ericsson proposes prioritizing in-band and intra-band operation in
Release-20 and a new specification for the topology involving an intermediate
UE, considering future band-combination expansion
[R4-2611721](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_120/Docs/R4-2611721.zip).

Both workstreams resist the assumption that a feature is finished once its
basic signal path is drawn. Coexistence, switching and receiver limits
still determine where the architecture can operate.

## Sensing: communications hardware is not automatically a radar

Nokia identifies a physical obstacle to monostatic sensing, where
transmission and reception are at the same site: legacy time-division-duplex
switching can create an excessive blind range while the receiver is
unavailable. It asks RAN4 to study switching and alternatives such as
separate transmit and receive antennas. For bistatic sensing, with separated
endpoints, synchronization accuracy becomes a distance-estimation constraint
[R4-2611858](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_120/Docs/R4-2611858.zip).

Ericsson asks integrated sensing and communication studies to protect
normal radio-resource-management (RRM) behavior as well as define sensing
accuracy. Weak reflected signals may need different measurement conditions;
UE sensing in uplink resources also raises UE-to-UE interference. It
proposes mapping sensing objectives to RF requirements rather than assuming
communication tests establish sensing performance
[R4-2611879](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_120/Docs/R4-2611879.zip).

Samsung proposes considering both conducted and radiated tests. The local
extraction supports that proposal but not a detailed reconstruction of its
illustrated test setups
[R4-2610369](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_120/Docs/R4-2610369.zip).

Sensing feasibility cannot be read from communication bandwidth alone.
Switching, synchronization, receiver sensitivity, interference and test
architecture are part of the claim. These remain study questions in the
reviewed record, not finalized sensing requirements.

## Satellite: protection models and handover assumptions

T-Mobile and Rogers' August contribution reports approval of the June
direction to study non-terrestrial-network (NTN) interference into
terrestrial downlinks, including aggregate interference from multiple
satellites and noise-limited coverage edges. Their new proposals distinguish
aggregate receiver protection from a universal emission limit on each
satellite
[R4-2612133](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_120/Docs/R4-2612133.zip).

SpaceX proposes constellation visibility and loading assumptions,
coverage-edge simulation choices, and testability assessment before new
satellite leakage requirements. Ericsson separately asks how to identify
vulnerable terrestrial users, noting that they need not simply be the users
at the fifth percentile of throughput
[R4-2610133](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_120/Docs/R4-2610133.zip),
[R4-2611464](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_120/Docs/R4-2611464.zip).

There is also a concrete mobility dispute. SpaceX proposes assuming a
global-navigation-satellite-system (GNSS) receiver is activated before
LTE-to-NR-NTN handover, removing acquisition time from the handover budget.
Qualcomm proposes a conditional zero acquisition-time treatment based on
recent measurements while leaving activation timing undefined
[R4-2610131](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_120/Docs/R4-2610131.zip),
[R4-2612187](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_120/Docs/R4-2612187.zip).

The protection model determines which users a coexistence test represents;
the handover assumptions determine which preparation costs its timing budget
includes. Neither dispute has a verified final August disposition in the
reviewed record. The [companion SpaceX analysis](/posts/nr-femto-starlink-small-cells/)
examines the terrestrial-band and small-cell implications separately.

## What the next evidence needs to settle

The August material supports a broad account of engineering choices and
company positions, not yet a comprehensive account of what RAN4 finally
approved. Earlier agreements cited inside proposals must retain their
earlier dates; submission for approval is not approval.

The next review should seek final dispositions and revised contributions
across these fronts:

- Spectrum and waveforms: whether operator continuity and sharing
  proposals entered agreed text; whether waveform comparisons align
  amplifier behavior, signal-quality limits and spectrum costs.
- Mobility and receiver tests: which interruption-control assumptions
  remain live, how processing time is counted, and whether receiver
  baselines and test operating points are reproducible across companies.
- AI: what model representation and test-platform constraints were
  settled, and whether mismatch testing and fallback became explicit study
  requirements.
- Release-20 implementation: agreed SBFD coexistence assumptions and
  the RAN1 inputs needed to complete Ambient IoT RF requirements.
- Sensing and satellite: which feasibility and protection models were
  aligned, and which numerical or procedural requirements remain proposals.

Read together, these sources show a recurring tension: flexibility creates
dependencies that specifications must make explicit. Spectrum reuse depends
on alignment; gap-less measurements on interruption behavior; AI-assisted
RF on generalization; sensing and full duplex on realizable isolation and
timing. The next milestone is turning those dependencies into requirements
that different vendors can implement and independent laboratories can test.
