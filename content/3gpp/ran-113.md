September's plenary in Madrid brought the August radio debates closer to
product decisions. Which capabilities must every device implement? How can
5G and 6G coexist without duplicating the whole network? When does an
improvement justify another round of specifications, testing and device
complexity? The answers matter before silicon and deployment plans harden.

The clearest migration instruction is unusually constrained: the outgoing
Radio Access Network (RAN) liaison asks for feasibility work on concurrent
5G voice and 6G data through two registrations, with no core-network traffic
aggregation or splitting. Meanwhile, the final device-type moderator text
sets out three reference types, and the transmitter-relaxation conclusion
makes further work conditional on demonstrated 6G gains. These are different
ways of narrowing the implementation problem
[RP-262264](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/TSG_RAN/TSGR_113/Docs/RP-262264.zip),
[RP-262256](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/TSG_RAN/TSGR_113/Docs/RP-262256.zip),
[RP-262258](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/TSG_RAN/TSGR_113/Docs/RP-262258.zip).

The same discipline appears in satellite work. SpaceX and its partners want
systems serving ordinary phones to accommodate standardized satellite
devices; the proposed work separates a feasibility checkpoint from later
normative requirements. Satellite use of terrestrial bands remains divided.
The important development is therefore the emerging shape of the work and
its conditions, rather than a blanket claim that Madrid settled 6G.

This report follows RAN#113, held on 14-17 September 2026, using primary text
available by 28 September. An outgoing liaison establishes a working-group
position; explicit session conclusions establish recorded decisions; a
work-item description establishes its submitted scope. Those evidence levels
are distinguished below. Missing final records prevent an exhaustive list
of approved work items.

## Migration: a narrow route and a broader industrial demand

### 5G voice alongside 6G data

The outgoing liaison to the system-architecture groups gives a precise RAN
view of dual registration. It describes a device registered separately to
the 5G core through New Radio (NR) and to the 6G core through the 6G radio.
The target is simultaneous voice over NR on the 5G leg and data on the 6G
leg. It expects no RAN impact to be specified, excludes core-network
aggregation and traffic splitting, and allows network-controlled connected
mobility rather than device-controlled connected mobility under dual
registration
[RP-262264](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/TSG_RAN/TSGR_113/Docs/RP-262264.zip).

This is a request to determine feasibility, not an announcement that a
complete migration architecture has been approved. RAN asks the architecture
working group, SA2, to report by RAN#114 and expects the system-architecture
plenary to decide no later than SA#114. Nor does a statement about dual
registration settle all dual-connectivity or aggregation alternatives. The
final migration moderator document is unavailable in the reviewed extraction.

Apple's separate checkpoint contribution illustrates the pressure to narrow
the alternatives. It proposes a 6G RAN anchor aggregated with NR cells as
the remaining migration option, or no such option, and asks that
non-collocated 6G-to-6G aggregation reuse the migration architecture where
possible. These are Apple's proposals, distinct from the outgoing liaison
[RP-262203](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/TSG_RAN/TSGR_113/Docs/RP-262203.zip).

The commercial question is how much existing service functionality can be
retained without making the first 6G device support every possible pairing.
The liaison's restrictions make that tradeoff concrete. They also leave
questions of feasibility and final system design with the groups responsible
for resolving them.

### Factories need continuity beyond the handset

The 5G Alliance for Connected Industries and Automation supplies a different
timescale. Its liaison describes industrial use cases planned for ten to
twenty years and asks for simultaneous operation of 5G and 6G assets,
including participation in the same application. It wants efficient sharing
of locally available spectrum, continuity in industrial-network integration,
and a modest equipment and operational footprint when 6G is added
[RP-262197](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/TSG_RAN/TSGR_113/Docs/RP-262197.zip).

This extends the spectrum-sharing thread from August. For a factory,
migration is not satisfied merely because an individual phone can register
with two cores. Time-sensitive communication, synchronization, configuration
and monitoring must continue across a mixed installation. The liaison is an
industry request rather than a 3GPP decision, but it explains why a clean
generational break would leave a substantial deployment problem unsolved.

## Reference device types: the minimum becomes a design choice

The device-type moderator summary evolves across several rounds. Its final
section describes at least three minimum mandatory feature sets, using
massive Internet of Things (IoT), enhanced mobile broadband and fixed
wireless access as the example use cases. Each user equipment (UE) belongs
to one reference device type, with additionally supported capabilities still
needing detail. Earlier rounds proposing only two types should not be
mistaken for the final text
[RP-262256](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/TSG_RAN/TSGR_113/Docs/RP-262256.zip).

The minimum parameters include transmit and receive antenna or chain counts
and a minimum supported maximum bandwidth in each direction. That awkward
bandwidth phrase describes a real distinction: a device may support a
maximum channel bandwidth, while the reference type imposes a floor on that
capability. The final section retains brackets around its wording and leaves
the detailed values and possible additional types for further discussion.

The summary directs further discussion toward RAN#114, keeps relevant
decisions at plenary level and allows RAN4 to continue work on broadband
minimum requirements. Its final section is itself labeled a proposal; the
report therefore supports describing the final moderator framework, without
pretending to possess a complete approval record for every parameter.

The consequence for Gothia cell, coding and receiver design is substantial.
A feature that is practical for a fixed terminal need not be the minimum
for a battery-powered sensor or handheld. Conversely, excessive device
categories can fragment testing and interoperability. The reference-type
framework is where those competing pressures have to be reconciled.

## Shaping and transmitter gains: separate the decisions

### Apple says the shaping checkpoint is resolved

The August RAN1 report described disagreement over whether constellation
shaping gains survive realistic link adaptation and receiver assumptions.
Apple's September checkpoint submission now characterizes the issue as
resolved: it says constellation shaping is not pursued in Rel-20 or Rel-21,
and proposes avoiding further plenary discussion of that checkpoint. It also
says downlink 4096-point quadrature amplitude modulation (QAM) is not pursued
in those releases and limits uplink 1024-QAM to fixed wireless access
[RP-262203](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/TSG_RAN/TSGR_113/Docs/RP-262203.zip).

That is significant new evidence, but its source matters. This is an Apple
submission, not the final plenary record. The extracted slides omit embedded
supporting agreement material. The database also lists September shaping
contributions by Qualcomm, T-Mobile and DeepSig whose text is unavailable.
Their titles establish continued participation in the topic; they cannot
establish a counter-position or overturn Apple's account.

The appropriate update is therefore that a prominent participant reports
the checkpoint closed, with the final disposition still to verify. Neither
continuing the August debate as though nothing changed nor declaring an
independently verified standards rejection would represent the evidence
properly. The record also does not justify importing a broader claim that
all waveform enhancements have ended.

### Receiver-assisted power gains remain conditional

Digital post-distortion is a separate mechanism: an advanced base-station
receiver compensates transmitter distortion, potentially allowing the UE to
back off its power less. The moderator's September conclusion places this
as a potential Rel-21 6G objective, assuming the study completes and shows
gain. Possible transfer to Rel-21 5G-Advanced depends on the design being
transferable, without reliance on 1024-QAM or a specifically 6G air-interface
feature
[RP-262258](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/TSG_RAN/TSGR_113/Docs/RP-262258.zip).

The submitted technical scope discusses transmitter error-vector magnitude
up to 6%, reduced maximum power reduction, specified UE power classes and
base-station demodulation requirements using a radio-frequency (RF) modeled stimulus. These
are proposed objectives, not a blanket new transmitter allowance. The
conclusion is also about post-distortion without artificial intelligence (AI); it must not be reported as
approval of the AI compensation schemes examined in the August RAN4 report.

The company's preferred release route and the engineering feasibility are
separate issues. Huawei, MediaTek, vivo and others appear under earlier
delivery alternatives in the annex; other participants favor further study
or a 6G route. The useful outcome is the condition attached to progression:
demonstrated gain first, with reuse in 5G dependent on the actual design.

## Gothia cell and spectrum sharing: keep the threads distinct

The September extraction supplies no new Gothia-cell outcome. The August
Single Cell Multi-Carrier (SCMC) debate therefore remains the latest verified
technical baseline. It asks whether several carriers can share a cell
context, and what additional coordination that entails. It should not be
conflated with either 5G/6G spectrum sharing or registration with two cores.

vivo identifies switching, simultaneous reception, phase continuity and
capability signaling as the important consequences of coordinating known
radio operations under one cell. Ericsson argues that per-carrier RF
requirements should remain those of carrier aggregation and questions
whether the limited scenarios for a single modulation-and-coding choice
justify the new concept
[R4-2611103](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_120/Docs/R4-2611103.zip),
[R4-2611826](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_120/Docs/R4-2611826.zip).

The distinct spectrum-sharing question is how two radio generations occupy
the same holdings. August's joint operator/Ericsson contribution sought
resource-block alignment between NR and 6G; the industrial liaison now
reinforces the deployment need for efficient local coexistence
[R4-2611987](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG4_Radio/TSGR4_120/Docs/R4-2611987.zip),
[RP-262197](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/TSG_RAN/TSGR_113/Docs/RP-262197.zip).

The next useful Gothia result would connect a defined deployment scenario
to a measurable advantage and an implementable capability contract. No new
outcome in the extracted September material means the question remains
open in this report; it does not mean the meeting abandoned the topic.

## AI and mobility: candidate work is not an approved package

The 6G requirements-maintenance summary records a narrow set of actual
session decisions. Editorial changes to the requirements report were
accepted. A proposal that UE-side collection should allow vendor-specific
data was discarded, as were two AI-related text proposals. These decisions
concern those proposed additions, not a prohibition on AI or all
vendor-specific information throughout 6G
[RP-262235](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/TSG_RAN/TSGR_113/Docs/RP-262235.zip).

This is a useful counterweight to treating every AI submission as an
expansion of the standard. The report's final agreement lists the editorial
change. Its discarded coverage proposal also has a narrower successor:
Lenovo asks RAN1 to identify which channels or signals would need relaxed
requirements to meet the 153 dB maximum-coupling-loss target. That later
way-forward submission is a proposal, not proof of acceptance
[RP-262247](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/TSG_RAN/TSGR_113/Docs/RP-262247.zip).

The Rel-21 non-satellite moderator summary requires similar care. Early
rounds include an explicit AI-for-the-air-interface candidate. The final
collected-input list instead names multiple-antenna and spectral-efficiency
enhancements, Ambient IoT, AI-assisted mobility, mobile-AI traffic and
quality-of-service adaptation, conventional mobility improvements, network
AI, antenna-control modernization and network-optimization work. Reading
the first round as the final package would add something the final list does
not contain
[RP-262244](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/TSG_RAN/TSGR_113/Docs/RP-262244.zip).

Those are inputs collected for further prioritization, not an approved
implementation schedule. Antenna-management modernization is worth retaining
as a tracked topic alongside the air interface: the discussion includes
infrastructure awareness, telemetry, diagnostics and existing interface gaps.
It remains a candidate whose scope and commercial justification need detail.

There is also a concrete mobility-capability proposal. The monitoring
work-item description seeks to increase NR inter-frequency monitoring
beyond the existing minimum of seven carriers and address the total-carrier
requirement of fourteen. Its scope is idle and inactive operation, excludes
connected mode and increased inter-radio-technology monitoring, and makes
the enhanced UE feature optional. It targets Rel-20 onwards without release
independence back to Rel-19
[RP-262248](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/TSG_RAN/TSGR_113/Docs/RP-262248.zip).

The question behind these numbers is how a device keeps track of increasingly
fragmented deployments without letting measurement delays grow unchecked.
The proposal includes scaling factors and backward-compatible requirements.
It turns the spectrum discussion into a practical measurement obligation,
while leaving final adoption to the approval record.

## Ambient IoT: a clearer boundary around the reader

The revised Release-20 Ambient IoT description provides unusually concrete
limits. The outdoor active Device C has a maximum transmit power of 5 dBm;
the indoor active Device 2b has a maximum of -10 dBm. The latter is to reuse
Device C's air interface with minimized differences, rather than create an
independent signal, channel and sequence design
[RP-262237](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/TSG_RAN/TSGR_113/Docs/RP-262237.zip).

For the topology in which a UE acts as reader, the description specifies a
connected reader, radio resources valid within its serving cell and resource
allocation through radio-resource-control signaling. It excludes coordination
between UE readers, new cellular-interface protocol layers and simultaneous
same-band operation of the cellular link and Ambient IoT at that reader.
The submitted scope thus directly addresses the shared-hardware questions
raised in August.

It also defers starting the device-originated autonomous-traffic work for
this topology until sufficient progress exists for the direct-reader
topology, following a December plenary checkpoint. The scope is detailed
enough to explain dependencies; its status as a revised work-item submission
does not independently certify every objective's approval. For implementers,
the exclusion of simultaneous same-band operation is especially relevant:
the reader role must fit around its ordinary cellular operation.

## Satellite: coexistence, compensation and capacity

### Terrestrial bands remain disputed

SpaceX's moderator summary explicitly says companies remain divided over
approaches to normative work on satellite use of terrestrial bands. It
records an alternative proposal to stop RAN discussion and wait for the
relevant 2027 World Radiocommunication Conference outcome. The entire
document is marked informational
[RP-262246](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/TSG_RAN/TSGR_113/Docs/RP-262246.zip).

The extracted position table is missing. It would be inappropriate to assign
the stop-and-wait proposal to specific companies from this text alone, or
to call SpaceX's moderator role evidence that its preferred approach won.
The supported update is that disagreement persisted through September.
No small-cell deployment announcement or new spectrum authorization follows
from this discussion.

### The compensation proposal addresses a migration problem

The downlink-compensation work is more technically specific. Qualcomm's
moderator summary names a joint initiating contribution from SpaceX, Apple,
Boost Mobile Network, Charter, EchoStar, KDDI, LG, MediaTek, Qualcomm, Rogers
and Telstra. It describes a wish to serve devices that do not perform
satellite uplink pre-compensation alongside standardized NR satellite
devices in the same cell
[RP-262224](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/TSG_RAN/TSGR_113/Docs/RP-262224.zip).

Network compensation makes timing and Doppler manageable around a reference
point, but residual errors vary across the footprint. A capable device can
compensate its residual error; an ordinary device cannot necessarily do so.
The other barrier is procedural: existing satellite access restrictions
need examination if the two populations are to share the cell. The
moderator attributes mid-2027 deployment urgency to the proponents; this
is their stated commercial motivation, not a verified launch commitment.

The later work-item description is explicitly staged. It calls for a study
checkpoint at RAN#114 before deciding whether and how to proceed to
normative work. One technique studies timing and frequency compensation;
another studies frequency compensation. They have equal priority, and
other techniques are not precluded
[RP-262263](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/TSG_RAN/TSGR_113/Docs/RP-262263.zip).

Its limits matter: quasi-earth-fixed cells, the specified lower-frequency
NR satellite bands, and optional enhancement support in addition to
mandatory Rel-17 satellite functionality. RAN4 and normative objectives
are to be added at the December checkpoint. This is a proposed compatibility
path between device populations, not proof that all ordinary phones can
join all standardized satellite networks.

### Power and aggregation acquire more specific scope

The revised satellite-requirements description explicitly includes
power-class-1.5 two-transmitter single-layer uplink multiple-input
multiple-output (MIMO) and transmit diversity, while excluding dual-layer
uplink MIMO. It also retains two downlink carriers and one uplink carrier
within a satellite access node, with 400+400 MHz contiguous Ka-band and
5+5 MHz non-contiguous L-band examples
[RP-262261](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/TSG_RAN/TSGR_113/Docs/RP-262261.zip).

This is a more specific submitted scope than the August debate. It does
not establish unrestricted aggregation across any bandwidth or satellite
combination. The accompanying moderator summary documents the argument
over transmit diversity versus single-layer coherent MIMO and reports
agreement to reuse applicable terrestrial power-class requirements
[RP-262238](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/TSG_RAN/TSGR_113/Docs/RP-262238.zip).

For Rel-21, the RAN4-led satellite discussion is still a prioritization
exercise. SpaceX requests inter-band aggregation with one uplink and two
downlink carriers, using n255+n256 as an example, and supports prioritizing
downlink aggregation over uplink aggregation. Other satellite operators
seek different carrier counts, bands and terminal cases. The final moderator
proposal asks proponents to quantify what existing specifications cannot
deliver, the performance penalty of existing mechanisms, and alignment
among operators, infrastructure, device and chipset suppliers
[RP-262245](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/TSG_RAN/TSGR_113/Docs/RP-262245.zip).

That connects directly to the terrestrial debate: an operator's fragmented
spectrum creates demand for aggregation, but the supported combinations,
terminal architecture and test burden still have to be justified. The
moderator's requests are a useful guide to what to examine next, rather
than evidence that every candidate feature entered Rel-21.

## GNSS resilience: agreements that sharpen the August record

Thales' September status report explicitly records the August decisions on
global navigation satellite system (GNSS) resilience. For initial access
in quasi-earth-fixed cells, RAN1 supports a reference-location approach
with satellite ephemeris. For earth-moving cells, it supports common
timing-advance and Doppler assistance. The association of assistance with
synchronization-signal beams still requires detail
[RP-262190](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/TSG_RAN/TSGR_113/Docs/RP-262190.zip).

The report records work on frequency-adjustment commands in the random-access
response, extending timing-adjustment range and transmitting two random-access
sequences with different roots. It leaves the arrangement of their occasions
and the command granularity for further decisions. These are concrete
protocol baselines, not merely alternative company proposals.

RAN4's recorded agreements separate initial-access transmit timing from
connected-mode timing. Its radio-resource-management work considers both
network assistance and a recently acquired location that remains sufficiently
valid. Fixed satellite terminals are excluded from baseline updates because
they are assumed to have location information; mobile terminals and UEs
remain in scope. The RF frequency-error work has a narrower initial focus
on network assistance, with other conditions still for study.

The status report also requests extra RAN4 time in August 2027 for
performance work expected to finish in September 2027. That is a rapporteur's
resource request and expectation. It should not be converted into an
unconditional promise that the feature is complete or commercially available
on that date.

## What October and December need to resolve

The next meetings have identifiable technical questions. The compensation
work-item proposal seeks early progress at RAN1#126bis on coexistence of
device populations before the December checkpoint. GNSS resilience needs
the remaining random-access choices and frequency-command granularity.
For Gothia cell, the evidence still needed is a supported deployment case,
benefit and corresponding RF and capability requirements.

Two further items deserve explicit follow-up. RAN1's post-August coverage
parameter discussion says it could not stabilize the higher-layer parameters
and therefore sent no liaison to RAN2. The new sounding-reference-signal
discussion defines evaluation methods but its extracted result tables are
empty; it cannot establish a winning sequence design
[R1-2606951](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_126/Docs/R1-2606951.zip),
[R1-2606950](https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/tsg_ran/WG1_RL1/TSGR1_126/Docs/R1-2606950.zip).

December's RAN#114, listed in the outgoing liaison for 7-11 December in
Boston, is the next explicit convergence point for migration feasibility,
device-type detail and proposed satellite compensation work. Rel-21
proponents also need sharper technical objectives and credible deployment
needs. The key reporting task is to check which conditions have been met,
which proposals became agreed text and which apparent closures still rely
on a participant's account.

The September record improves the August story in both directions. It adds
specific GNSS agreements and tighter satellite scope, while revealing that
some broad AI additions were discarded and some claimed decisions still
need corroboration. A useful account of 6G progress must preserve both:
the designs gaining definition and the boundaries that keep their first
implementations feasible.
